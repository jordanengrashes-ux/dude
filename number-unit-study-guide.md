# Number Unit Study Guide

Use this to prepare for the unit test. Part 1 is vocabulary. Part 2 is the skills, with worked examples. Part 3 is a practice set with answers.

---

## Part 1: Vocabulary

### Place value and digits
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **number** | An amount or quantity, written with one or more digits (e.g., 4 306). |
| **digit** | One of the symbols 0–9 used to write numbers. 4 306 has four digits. |
| **place value** | The value a digit has because of its position in a number. |
| **value** | What a digit is worth in its position (digit × place value). In 4 306, the value of the 3 is 300. |
| **ones, thousands, millions, billions, trillions** | Place-value periods. Each period has three places (hundreds, tens, ones). Each period is 1 000 times the one before it. |
| **magnitude** | How big a number is, usually described by its power of 10 (e.g., 10⁶ is of the order of millions). |
| **written form** | A number written in words, e.g., 2 305 = "two thousand three hundred five". |

**Place value chart**

| trillions | billions | millions | thousands | ones |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H T O | H T O | H T O | H T O | H T O |

**Decimal places:** 0.**3** tenths · 0.0**4** hundredths · 0.00**5** thousandths. Read 0.352 as "three hundred fifty-two thousandths".

### Scientific notation
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **power of 10** | 10 multiplied by itself: 10³ = 1 000, 10⁻² = 0.01. |
| **exponent** | The small raised number telling how many times the base is used. In 10⁴ the exponent is 4. |
| **scientific notation** | A number written as a × 10ⁿ, where 1 ≤ a < 10 and n is an integer. |
| **scientific expanded notation** | The decimal part is written out and multiplied by the power of 10 in full, e.g., 6.4 × 10⁵ = 6.4 × 100 000. (Show the power of 10 as its standard number, then multiply.) |

### Comparing
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **compare** | Decide which number is larger, smaller or the same. |
| **greater than (>)** | 9 > 4 |
| **less than (<)** | 4 < 9 |
| **equal to (=)** | 5 = 5 |

### Kinds of numbers
| Term | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **natural numbers** | Counting numbers | 1, 2, 3, … |
| **whole numbers** | Natural numbers and 0 | 0, 1, 2, 3, … |
| **integers** (singular **integer**) | Whole numbers and their negatives | …, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, … |
| **rational number** | Can be written as a fraction a/b of two integers (b ≠ 0). Its decimal either stops or repeats. | 3/4, −2, 0.25, 0.333… |
| **irrational number** | Cannot be written as a fraction. Its decimal never stops and never repeats. | π, √2, √7 |
| **real numbers** | All rational and irrational numbers; every point on the number line. | |
| **terminating decimal** | A decimal that ends. | 0.75, 2.5 |
| **repeating decimal** | A decimal with a digit or group that repeats forever. | 0.333… = 0.3̅, 0.1666… |

**Nesting:** natural ⊂ whole ⊂ integers ⊂ rational ⊂ real. Irrational numbers are also real, but not rational.

### Factors and multiples
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **factor** | A number that divides another exactly. Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. |
| **common factor** | A factor shared by two or more numbers. Common factors of 12 and 18: 1, 2, 3, 6. |
| **multiple** | The product of a number and a whole number. Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, … |
| **common multiple** | A multiple shared by two or more numbers. Common multiples of 4 and 6: 12, 24, 36, … |
| **prime number** | Exactly two factors: 1 and itself. 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 … |
| **composite number** | More than two factors. 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12 … |

Notes: 1 is neither prime nor composite. 2 is the only even prime. 0 is neither.

### Roots and estimating
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **number line** | A line on which numbers are placed in order, equally spaced. |
| **square root** (√) | A number that, multiplied by itself, gives the original. √49 = 7 because 7 × 7 = 49. |
| **perfect square** | A number whose square root is a whole number: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144 … |
| **estimate** | A careful guess that is close to the exact answer. |

## Part 2: Skills

### 1. Value and place value of a digit
In **7 482 316**:
- The **place value** of the 4 is hundred thousands.
- The **value** of the 4 is 400 000.
- The value of the 7 is 7 000 000; the value of the 8 is 80 000.

*Place value* = the name of the position. *Value* = the digit × that position.

### 2. Identify or determine a number
"6 in the millions, 2 in the thousands, 5 in the tens, 0 elsewhere" → 6 000 000 + 2 000 + 50 = **6 002 050**. Fill in zeros for unnamed places.

### 3. Written form
- 45 308 → forty-five thousand three hundred eight.
- 3 004 020 → three million four thousand twenty.
- 0.407 → four hundred seven thousandths.
- Don't say "and" in whole numbers; "and" is the decimal point (12.5 → "twelve and five tenths").

### 4. Standard → scientific notation
Move the decimal point until there is one non-zero digit in front of it. Count the moves.
- **Big number** → positive exponent.
  64 920 800 000 000 → move the decimal 13 places left → **6.49208 × 10¹³**
- **Small number** → negative exponent.
  0.0000064 → move 6 places right → **6.4 × 10⁻⁶**

### 5. Standard → scientific expanded notation
Write the scientific notation, then replace the power of 10 with its full value.
- 3 500 000 → 3.5 × 10⁶ → **3.5 × 1 000 000**
- 0.00072 → 7.2 × 10⁻⁴ → **7.2 × 0.0001**
  (or 7.2 ÷ 10 000)

### 6. Scientific (expanded) → standard
Move the decimal the number of places the exponent says: right for positive, left for negative.
- 4.8 × 10⁵ = 480 000
- 2.05 × 10⁻³ = 0.00205
- 9.1 × 1 000 000 = 9 100 000

### 7. Comparing numbers in scientific notation
1. Compare the **exponents** first. Bigger exponent → bigger number (for positive numbers).
2. If the exponents are equal, compare the decimal parts.
   - 3.2 × 10⁸ > 9.9 × 10⁷ (8 > 7)
   - 5.4 × 10⁻³ > 5.4 × 10⁻⁵ (−3 > −5)
   - 6.1 × 10⁴ < 6.7 × 10⁴ (same exponent, 6.1 < 6.7)

### 8. Decimals to the thousandths
- 4.**3**07: ones 4, tenths 3, hundredths 0, thousandths 7.
- Read 2.058 as "two and fifty-eight thousandths".
- Line up decimal points to compare: 0.45 vs 0.405 → 0.450 vs 0.405 → 0.45 is greater.

### 9. Classify numbers
Ask in this order:
1. Does the decimal **end or repeat**? → rational. Never ends, never repeats → irrational.
2. Is it a fraction of integers? → rational.
3. Is it a square root of a non-perfect square? → irrational.

### 10. Prime or composite
Test divisibility by 2, 3, 5, 7 (only primes up to the square root are needed).
- 29: not divisible by 2, 3 or 5 → **prime**.
- 51: 5 + 1 = 6, divisible by 3 → 51 = 3 × 17 → **composite**.

Divisibility rules: 2 (ends in even digit), 3 (digit sum divisible by 3), 5 (ends in 0 or 5), 9 (digit sum divisible by 9), 10 (ends in 0).

### 11. Common factors and common multiples
- Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
- Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36
- **Common factors:** 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 (greatest = 12)
- Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36 …
- Multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24, 32, 40 …
- **Common multiples:** 24, 48, … (least = 24)

### 12. Compare and order real numbers on a number line
Convert to decimals (or estimate square roots) so they are comparable.
- √5 ≈ 2.24, 9/4 = 2.25, 2.2
- Order least → greatest: 2.2, √5, 9/4
- Negatives flip: −√5 ≈ −2.24 is **less** than −2.2.

Mark each on a number line at its approximate decimal position, then read left to right (least → greatest).

### 13. Powers (exponents)
Exponent = repeated multiplication.
- 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32
- 5³ = 125
- 10⁴ = 10 000
- Any number to the power 1 is itself; any non-zero number to the power 0 is 1.
- Watch signs: (−2)² = 4, but −2² = −4. (−2)³ = −8.

### 14. Square roots of perfect squares
√81 = 9, √144 = 12, √0.25 = 0.5, √(4/9) = 2/3.

### 15. Estimate the square root of a non-perfect square
Find the two perfect squares on either side, then judge which is closer.
- √20: 16 < 20 < 25, so 4 < √20 < 5. 20 is 4 above 16 and 5 below 25, so it is a little closer to 16. **√20 ≈ 4.5** (actual 4.47).
- √50: 49 < 50 < 64 → just above 7 → **≈ 7.1** (actual 7.07).

Check by squaring your estimate: 4.5² = 20.25 ✔.

### 16. Positive and negative square roots
Every positive number has **two** square roots.
- √49 = 7 (the principal, or positive, root)
- −√49 = −7 (the negative root)
- x² = 49 → x = 7 **or** x = −7, written ±7.
- √(−49) is not a real number.

### 17. Order of operations — BEDMAS / PEMDAS
**B**rackets → **E**xponents (and roots) → **D**ivision and **M**ultiplication (left to right) → **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction (left to right).

Example: 6 + 2 × (9 − 4)² ÷ 5
1. Brackets: 9 − 4 = 5 → 6 + 2 × 5² ÷ 5
2. Exponent: 5² = 25 → 6 + 2 × 25 ÷ 5
3. × and ÷ left to right: 2 × 25 = 50, 50 ÷ 5 = 10 → 6 + 10
4. Add: **16**

### 18. Word problems — strategies
1. **Read** twice; underline the question.
2. **Pick out** the numbers and what each means.
3. **Choose** an operation or tool (diagram, table, number line, list of factors).
4. **Solve**, showing each step.
5. **Check** that the answer is reasonable and has the right units, and answer in a sentence.

Typical types in this unit:
- *Scientific notation:* "Earth is about 1.5 × 10⁸ km from the Sun; a nucleus is 1 × 10⁻¹⁵ m wide. Which is bigger?"
- *Common multiples:* "Bus A comes every 12 min and Bus B every 18 min. If both leave at 8:00, when do they next leave together?" → LCM of 12 and 18 = 36 → 8:36.
- *Common factors:* "Share 24 pencils and 36 erasers into identical kits with nothing left over. Most kits?" → GCF = 12 kits.
- *Square roots:* "A square garden has area 81 m². What is the side length?" → √81 = 9 m.
